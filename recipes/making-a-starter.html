Making a Sourdough Starter from Scratch

No friend’s starter handy? Grow your own. Flour, water and about a week of patience — here’s how.

To grow a starter you don’t need much, and it takes longer than people like to admit — a week at least. The good news: almost none of that time is work. Here’s how I’d start one today.

What you’ll need

A clean jar
1
Whole wheat flour
100g
Water
100g
Kitchen scale
1

The day-by-day

A starter is a small colony of wild yeast and bacteria. You feed it, it grows, and the good microbes crowd out the bad. Each day you’ll do the same short chore: throw out half, add 50g flour and 50g water, stir. That’s it.

  1. Day 1 · Mix

    Put 50g whole wheat flour and 50g water in the jar and stir until no dry flour is left. Mark the level with the rubber band, so you can see it rise later. Lid on loosely — it needs air, not a seal. Nothing happens today. That’s normal.

  2. Day 2 · Feeding time

    Throw away about half, then add 50g flour and 50g water and stir. A few bubbles may appear. The smell is still innocent, almost sweet — that changes soon.

  3. Day 3 · The great leap (that isn’t)

    It may suddenly look very alive, then just as suddenly look dead — a flat grey mush. Both are fine. Keep feeding: discard half, 50g flour, 50g water. Don’t start over. This is exactly the moment everyone gives up.

  4. Day 4 · A rhythm

    Same sad-sounding chore: discard half, feed 50/50. Now you start to notice a rhythm, a rise and a fall after each feed. The smell turns properly sour. That’s the microbes talking — it’s good.

  5. Day 5–7 · Patience

    Keep the daily 1:1:1 feed going. What you’re after is a steady doubling within a few hours and a surface dotted with bubbles. Mine only truly clicked around day 8–10. So did lots of other people’s. Yours isn’t broken.

When is it active?

An active starter doubles within a few hours of a feed, the top is foamy with bubbles, and it smells pleasantly sour — not sharp, not like nail-polish remover. That’s the same “active” the general method talks about.

From there you can set your first loaf going. Keep the extra starter in the fridge between bakes — it pauses, stays alive, and it’ll happily wait weeks for you.

Common problems & fixes

No bubbles yet

It’s early days until about day 3, and a cold kitchen slows things right down. Move it somewhere warmer and give it time before you judge it.

Smells sharp, like nail-polish remover

It’s hungry — the culture is bigger than what you’re feeding it. Feed more often, or give it a slightly bigger feed (say 75g flour and 75g water).

A grey liquid pooling on top (hooch)

Same story: it’s hungry. Pour the hooch off and feed as usual. It’s a sign to feed more often, not a dead starter.

Mould — fuzzy, coloured, on top

That’s the one you don’t fix. Throw it out and start again. It costs two ingredients and a few days, and you’ve already learned the moves.

In the end, a starter is just flour, water and a week of looking after it — and it’s the start of every loaf on this site.
